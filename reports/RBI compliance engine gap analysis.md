# Closing the gap between RBI rules and complaint engines

**No product or open-source repository found in this research turns RBI's complaint rules into executable, dated, clause-cited checks on individual complaints, and CustomerPulse's 17-rule engine does not either.** As of 12 October 2026 the binding framework is the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2026 (in force 1 July 2026), the Internal Ombudsman (IO) Directions 2026 (14 January 2026), the Responsible Business Conduct (RBC-CB) Directions 2025 (updated to 1 October 2026), the 2019 failed-transaction TAT circular, and the fraud, KYC, cyber and AI rules around them. Together they require a fully automated complaint system with a 30-day reply clock, auto-escalation to the IO within 20 days, a three-state outcome classification, mandatory reply content, and clock-based compensation. Enforcement keeps catching banks on exactly these deterministic clocks: Union Bank of India itself was fined **₹95.40 lakh in March 2026** partly for not crediting disputed amounts within 10 days ([RBI PR](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=62454)). Commercial platforms (CRMNext, Newgen, Salesforce, Pega) offer generic SLA matrices, and the regulatory-intelligence tools (CUBE, Corlytics, Ascent, TeamLease RegTrack) stop at policy and control mapping. The RBI-specific GitHub projects are student-scale RAG wrappers with 0–10 stars. CustomerPulse has a real head start (a rules file, evidence storage, explanations, regulatory RAG), but code review shows its engine is mostly inert on live data: complaints look "resolved" the moment AI processing finishes, 9 of 17 rules can never fire, and evaluation happens once. The report lists **38 capabilities** an RBI Compliance Engine needs; CustomerPulse has none fully built, 13 partial and 25 missing, and no external product is known to have the full set.

## What RBI requires as of October 2026

The rulebook splits into a complaint-lifecycle core and a ring of adjacent regimes. RB-IOS 2026 replaced the 2021 scheme on 1 July 2026; complaints the Ombudsman received before that date stay under 2021 rules, so the engine must key the scheme version on the Ombudsman's receipt date (cl. 20) ([RB-IOS 2026 text](https://av.sc.com/in/content/docs/in-integrated-ombudsman-scheme.pdf)). A customer may escalate after **30 days** without a reply (or the longer RBI/NPCI/card-network TAT), and must file within **90 days** of that point or of the bank's last communication, replacing the old one-year window. Compensation caps rose to **₹30 lakh** (consequential loss) plus **₹3 lakh** (time, expense, harassment), up from ₹20 lakh and ₹1 lakh ([Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/01/rbi-integrated-ombudsman-scheme-2026-key-changes/)). The bank has 15 days to file its written response with the Ombudsman, and 30 days to comply with or appeal an accepted award. The IO Directions add the bank-side clocks: auto-escalate every partly resolved or wholly rejected complaint to the IO within **20 days of receipt** (or leave the IO at least 10 days before an external TAT), and communicate the final decision within **30 days of receipt**. The system may record only three outcomes, and a complaint may not be closed by the branch that handled it ([IO Directions 2026](https://sbi.bank.in/documents/53471/53742/11082026_Annexure+I-RBI+Commercial+Banks-Internal+Ombudsman+Directions+2026.pdf/7a497eab-60d3-155f-e5cd-357b80ebda24)). Upheld rejections must say the IO reviewed them and must give the Ombudsman route, the CMS URL and the CRPC address.

Three structural facts shape any design. First, the 28 November 2025 consolidation moved about 9,000 circulars into 244 entity-wise Master Directions "as-is", so a rule must cite the new instrument (RBC-CB, KYC Directions 2025), not the repealed 2016 or 2017 circulars ([SNG & Partners](https://sngpartners.in/2025/12/04/rbi-rolls-out-consolidated-master-directions/)). Second, RBI withdrew the 2021 grievance-strengthening circular on 11 February 2026 (RBI/2025-26/207), so disclosure rules tied to it must be dropped and mapped to the Financial Statements Master Direction, whose exact complaint table the notes could not retrieve ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-withdraws-circular-on-bank-grievance-redress-mechanism)). Third, RBC-CB has no generic acknowledgement or resolution TAT for all complaints; the 30-day clock is inherited from RB-IOS cl. 10(1)(f) and IO cl. 14(9). The three-day acknowledgement in CustomerPulse's RBI-ACK-001 therefore has no clause behind it in the notes.

Unresolved items the engine must carry as configurable parameters rather than constants: the RB-IOS 2026 circular number (not found), the NBFC/SFB/CIC variants of the IO Directions (a "25-day" CIC figure is unverified), the final 1 January 2027 liability parameters (a fetch of the RBI press release failed in this session, so the notes' "themes only" status stands), current NPCI dispute-stage TATs (not retrieved), and DPDP Rule 7 timing.

## Banks keep missing deterministic clocks, and regulators now say so publicly

The Ombudsman received **13.34 lakh complaints in FY2024-25, up 13.55%**; disposal fell to **93.07%** from 95.10%; loans and advances were 29.25% and credit cards rose 20.04% ([RBI Annual Report 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)). 37.84% of complaints at the regional offices were non-maintainable, and the largest reason (33%) was customers who had never complained to the bank first. About 2.45 lakh were auto-closed in the CMS. Of maintainable complaints, **51.9% ended in settlement or mediation**, meaning the bank conceded only once RBI was involved. Average turnaround for unauthorised digital-payment complaints reportedly rose from 36.3 days (FY23) to 68.3 days (FY25) ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/ai-enabled-tools-to-streamline-complaint-handling-at-rbi-mos-finance)). The FY2025-26 report is not yet published.

RBI's leadership has named the failure modes precisely. Governor Malhotra called misclassifying complaints as queries or requests "a gross regulatory violation" ([RBI speech](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1498)). Deputy Governor Swaminathan said in July 2026 that a "significant proportion" of complaints finally decided for the customer had never reached the IO, that closure is not resolution, and that "a hundred complaints relating to the same issue are seldom a hundred independent problems" ([RBI Bulletin](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=24304)). The Kanungo Committee had said the same in 2023: most rejected complaints were not referred to the IO and board customer-service committees were largely ineffective ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/will-rbi-governors-tough-talk-on-consumer-complaints-lead-to-real-change/76725.html)). Cost recovery makes it financial: **₹7.18 crore** was recovered from 31 banks for FY2024-25.

Penalties cluster on clock-based customer-protection rules rather than on exotic conduct. The sources show the 10-working-day shadow credit and 90-day compensation breached by SBI (₹1.728 crore, April 2025), Central Bank of India (₹1.455 crore, June 2024) and Union Bank of India (₹95.40 lakh, March 2026, which also lacked a 24x7 reporting channel). IO non-compliance drew penalties on RBL (₹2.27 crore) and Muthoot FinCorp; KYC drew HDFC (₹75 lakh) and IDFC First; recovery-agent call hours drew HDFC (reported ₹1 crore) and Kotak ([RBI SBI PR](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/press-releases/rbi-imposes-monetary-penalty-on-state-bank-of-india-1), [Central Bank PR](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=58104), [Business Standard on RBL](https://www.business-standard.com/article/finance/rbi-fines-rbl-bank-rs-2-27-cr-for-failure-on-internal-ombudsman-scheme-123032000958_1.html)). Penalties arrive 18–24 months after the inspection reference date, so a bank needs evidence that can be replayed years later. That argues for an immutable, clause-linked decision trail more than for better dashboards.

## Existing solutions automate workflow, not obligation

Indian banks run complaints on general case platforms. HDFC Bank's published policy describes CRMNext with category-level TATs, auto-escalation on elapsed time and reference numbers ([HDFC policy](https://v1.hdfcbank.com/assets/pdf/grievance_redressal_policy.pdf)). Newgen, Kapture and Salesforce sell case management with SLA routing; Salesforce's Agentforce deck lists a complaint agent that drafts "compliant responses" but gives no accuracy figures (vendor claim) ([Salesforce deck](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/Industry%20-%20Financial%20Services/EXTERNAL-260-Releasely-Slide-Deck-Agentforce-Financial-Services-.pdf)). Pega Smart Dispute is the closest analogue to what is missing: packaged, regulation-specific SLA packs for US Reg E/Reg Z, Canada and UK Section 75 ([Pega](https://pega.com/industries/financial-services/smart-dispute)). No vendor was found marketing an IO Directions 2026 or RB-IOS 2026 pack, three-state outcome handling, or RBI compensation calculation. The notes do not cover most other Indian vendors, so "none found" is the claim, not "none exist".

The regulatory-technology half models obligations as text, not executable logic. RBI's January 2024 circular (RBI/2023-24/117) requires workflow-based compliance monitoring tools ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/internal-compliance-monitoring-rbi-circular-117)); TeamLease RegTrack answers it with owners, periodic tasks and deviation approvals, and claims 1,536 Acts and 69,233 compliances (vendor claim) ([RegTrack](https://teamleaseregtech.com/product-services/regtrack-financial-institutions/)). CUBE, Corlytics and Ascent parse global rules into versioned obligation objects mapped to controls, with human validation; Corlytics claims 99%+ model accuracy against a ~98% human benchmark, with no independent audit ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/aml-tech-forum-usa/2025/12/11/can-data-make-sense-of-an-ever-changing-rulebook/)). Their explainability means showing source text to a reviewer, not a decision trace of rule, clause, inputs and outcome. RBI's own CMS is portal-based with no public bank-side API found, and its AI upgrade is staged as plans ([Regulation Asia](https://www.regulationasia.com/articles/rbi-to-roll-out-ai-tools-to-speed-up-complaint-handling)).

The open-source review (21 repositories cloned or read) found no RBI complaint engine. The RBI-specific repos are 0–10-star projects: ComplianceIQ's fixed-size chunking and a RAGAS evaluation fed citation labels instead of retrieved text; ComplyNext's clean deterministic NPA classifier (which covers loan classification, not complaints, and hard-codes thresholds); an ombudsman classifier scoring F1 = 1.0 on synthetic data; and ReguMind's empty `risk.service.js` ([ComplianceIQ](https://github.com/pavith-raj/ComplianceIQ/blob/main/backend/app/eval/run_eval.py), [ComplyNext](https://github.com/Nevin100/ComplyNext-Minor_Project/blob/main/backend/src/rules_engine/npa_engine.py), [metrics.json](https://github.com/Prasanthdj8/rbi-ombudsman-analytics/blob/main/classifier/metrics.json)). Most carry no license. The useful material is generic. OpenFisca (AGPL, so copy the pattern, not the code) keys parameter values and formulas by start date, carries references, and traces every computation ([variable.py](https://github.com/openfisca/openfisca-core/blob/master/openfisca_core/variables/variable.py)). The rule engines json-rules-engine, Microsoft RulesEngine and GoRules Zen return per-condition traces but have no effective dating or business-day arithmetic. The `holidays` library (MIT) offers working-day maths and Indian state calendars but does not model second and fourth Saturday closures or an RBI holiday list ([india.py](https://github.com/vacanza/holidays/blob/dev/holidays/countries/india.py)). Presidio (MIT) ships Aadhaar and PAN recognisers but no account, IFSC or UPI recognisers ([Presidio](https://github.com/data-privacy-stack/presidio/tree/main/presidio-analyzer/presidio_analyzer/predefined_recognizers/country_specific/india)).

The methods literature points the same way. Rules-as-code pilots (BIS Ellipse, FCA/BoE Digital Regulatory Reporting) reached proof of concept and concluded that a precise data dictionary must precede executable rules ([FCA review](https://fca.org.uk/firms/pa-consulting-digital-regulatory-reporting-review-phase-1-and-2-digital-regulatory-reporting)). In a Taiwanese enforcement study, an LLM alone scored F1 0.31 on compliance restoration while LLM plus an SMT solver scored 1.00, though on small problems; LLM-generated constraints were only 86.2% accurate ([arXiv 2601.06181](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06181v1)). Legal-research RAG tools still hallucinate 17–33% of the time ([arXiv 2405.20362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362v1)). The conclusion drawn here: LLMs should extract facts and draft text; dated deterministic rules should decide breaches, with every decision logged. Hinglish classification is weak (best model 74.41% accuracy on 14 classes) and no public Indian banking complaint dataset exists ([arXiv 2412.16614](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16614v1)), so CustomerPulse must build its own gold set.

## CustomerPulse's engine is mostly inert on live data

The notes' findings were checked against the code at commit 810f500 on this branch. Most are confirmed; one needs a narrower statement.

**Clocks are corrupted at the mapper.** `ProcessingService._compliance_input` sets `resolved_at = complaint.processed_at` whenever AI status is COMPLETED and also passes `acknowledged_at=complaint.processed_at` (`backend/app/services/processing_service.py:417`, `:431`). AI enrichment is treated as resolution and acknowledgement. Consequently the "unresolved over 30 days" rule (`engine.py:122-128`) and the customer-unresolved rule (`engine.py:177-184`) return early for any completed complaint, while the acknowledgement rule (`engine.py:96-102`) compares `processed_at` to `date_received + 3 days`, so it fires on every old import that is processed long after its received date. `sla_breached` is computed from `timely_response` or an unresolved-after-30-days test on the same distorted `resolved_at` (`processing_service.py:419`).

**Nine of 17 rules can never fire from the pipeline.** The mapper never sets `amount_disputed`, the KYC fields, `customer_notified_at`, `customer_service_breached`, the missing-document fields, `is_incomplete` or `missing_investigation_evidence`, and `AISignals` is built without `fraud_risk_score` (`processing_service.py:422-458`), although `RiskSignalService` computes a fraud score (`backend/app/intelligence/risk_signal_service.py:41`). The dead rules are RBI-HV-001, RBI-KYC-UPDATE/MISSING/VERIFY-001, RBI-CUST-NOTIFY-001, RBI-CUST-SERVICE-001, RBI-DOC-MANDATORY-001, RBI-DOC-INCOMPLETE-001 and RBI-DOC-EVIDENCE-001. The fraud rule still fires from text.

**Evaluation happens once.** `_store_compliance_evidence` returns without evaluating if any record already exists for the complaint (`processing_service.py:391-399`). There is no scheduler or lifecycle trigger, so a complaint's breach risk never changes as days pass.

**Keyword matching is substring-based and English-only.** `_legal_or_regulatory_keyword` tests `keyword in text` (`engine.py:107-109`); "court" matches "courtesy" and so triggers RBI-LEGAL-001. `_fraud_evidence` tests "fraud", "unauthorized" and "identity theft" (`engine.py:206-215`), so "not a fraud" triggers the fraud rule, while the Indian spelling "unauthorised", Hinglish, phishing, OTP and "ATM did not dispense cash" are missed. The amount used by RBI-HV-001 is never parsed from text such as "Rs 2,50,000".

**Authorisation is absent on the most dangerous endpoints.** In `compliance/router.py`, `POST /evaluate` (line 60), `DELETE /evidence/{id}` (line 199), `POST /rules` (242), `PUT /rules/{id}` (253) and `POST /reason-codes` (277) take no `require_roles` dependency, while the regulatory-document endpoints do (lines 290–419). Anyone able to reach the API can delete audit evidence or rewrite the rule catalogue.

**Two disconnected catalogues, and dates are ignored.** The engine reads `rules.json` (17 rules, no effective-date, version or source field; `rule_registry.py`), while the database holds `ComplianceRuleRecord` rows seeded in `seed_data.py` with different IDs (for example RBI-SLA-RESOLUTION-001, RBI-FRAUD-DELAY-001) and an `effective_from`/`effective_to` model (`models.py:275-303`). `ComplianceEngine.evaluate` never consults the database or any date, and hard-codes the 3-day, 7-day, 15-day and 30-day constants (`engine.py:97`, `:173`, `:181`).

**Regulatory RAG is thin and over-attributes.** The only regulation in storage is the RBI Lead Bank Scheme master circular (`storage/regulations/RBI/2019/…10MC02072019_1__2.md`), which does not govern complaints. The source search runs with `status=None` and `min_similarity=0.0` (`explainability/service.py:148-150`) and tags every returned citation as supporting every triggered rule (`:169`). `risk_justifier.py:15-20` infers severity by text-searching rule descriptions for "critical", "high" and similar words.

**Escalation is not wired to compliance.** `escalations/service.py:202-204` holds only a comment placeholder for a compliance check. There is no IO auto-escalation, no three-state outcome, and no Ombudsman route.

**Tests do not protect the mapper's semantics.** The compliance suite is large (104 passing tests at about 90% coverage, per the earlier verified session, not re-run here), but the only test touching `_compliance_input` asserts the response-field mapping (`tests/compliance/test_processing_compliance_trigger.py:149-158`). None checks `resolved_at`, `acknowledged_at`, the SLA flags or the unpopulated fields. This corrects the broader statement that no test touches the mapper.

## Target architecture and build order for CustomerPulse

The target is a deterministic core wrapped by LLM perception. Complaints from every channel pass PII masking, then a high-recall complaint-vs-query classifier and fact extractor (Bedrock with enum-constrained output, benchmarked against a TF-IDF baseline, since CFPB experiments showed a linear model matching zero-shot LLMs at much lower cost ([results](https://github.com/carmenwu0520/cfpb-complaint-nlp/blob/main/results_table.md))). Extracted facts and human-entered events land in an append-only event store carrying business time and record time. A versioned obligation registry (YAML/JSON in Git, OpenFisca-style dated parameters with circular references) is resolved by receipt date. A bank-calendar service computes due dates; an evaluator runs on every event and on a scheduler tick, emits a per-condition trace and persists it hash-chained; compensation calculators produce amounts with their formulas. Workflow actions (IO escalation, response QA gate, fraud-case spawn, Ombudsman file preparation) consume evaluator output; the RAG layer only explains and cites, filtered to texts in force on the relevant date. This design follows the note-level evidence that vendors lack exactly these joins, and adopts the "LLM extracts, rules decide" split supported by the neuro-symbolic results above.

Priorities, in order. **Phase 0 (days; fix what is wrong today):** put `require_roles` on `/evaluate`, evidence delete, rule and reason-code writes; stop mapping `processed_at` into `resolved_at` and `acknowledged_at` and use null until a real event exists; pass `RiskSignalService`'s fraud score; replace substring tests with word-boundary and negation-aware matching plus Indian spellings; allow re-evaluation instead of the existing-record return; add mapper tests that assert dates and flags. **Phase 1 (weeks; the regulatory spine):** unify rules into one dated registry with source clauses and lint; add the complaint event table; build the bank calendar on `holidays` and an RBI-IOS pack (R2, R3, R6, R7, R8, R9, R12–R14) with a gold timeline suite and a clock scheduler; wire escalation to the IO workflow and the three outcomes; load the actual RB-IOS 2026, IO Directions, RBC-CB and 2019 TAT texts into the corpus, add per-rule retrieval and a minimum similarity. **Phase 2 (months; intelligence and assurance):** fact extraction and Hinglish evaluation sets, the reply QA gate, fraud-case bridge and KYC checks, compensation calculators, clustering tied to breached clauses, hash-chained audit and legal hold, and model governance artefacts aligned to FREE-AI. **Phase 3:** the circular-change pipeline and the 1 January 2027 regime as future-dated rules, plus Board and CMS reconciliation reporting. The date pressure is concrete: R15's expanded fraud regime starts 1 January 2027, and DPDP breach rules about 13 May 2027.

## Conclusion

The gap is not missing AI. It is that no one has joined four plain things: dated, source-cited obligations; working-day clocks on an Indian bank calendar; re-evaluation as events and days accrue; and a trace that survives an inspection 18–24 months later. RBI's own statements describe this joint failure, since it is clocks, escalation and closure-versus-resolution that banks keep missing, and the Governor and Deputy Governor are asking for AI that supports human judgment rather than replacing it.

For CustomerPulse the immediate implication is uncomfortable but cheap to act on: before adding capability, the current pipeline's reported "compliance risk" is unreliable because the mapper manufactures resolution and acknowledgement and most rules are unreachable, so any demo figure derived from it should not be presented as regulatory assurance. After the Phase 0 repairs, the Phase 1 RBI-IOS rule pack is the differentiator, because nobody found sells it, and it can be built on small, permissively licensed parts.

**Caveats.** Limited verification (two calls) confirmed DPDP Rule 7's ~13 May 2027 timing only from secondary commentary; the RBI press release for the 1 January 2027 regime could not be fetched. Figures marked unverified in the table, vendor claims, the FY2025-26 Ombudsman data, the unread NBFC/CIC IO variants and the financial-statements complaint disclosure table remain open.
