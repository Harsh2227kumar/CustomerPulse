# Problems, Failures and Enforcement in Indian Bank Complaint Handling and RBI Compliance (2021-2026)

Research date: 12 Oct 2026. Latest Ombudsman annual report available is FY2024-25 (published 1 Dec 2025). The FY2025-26 report was not found; on past timing it would come out around Dec 2026.

## 1. Complaint volumes, trends, categories and outcomes (RBI Ombudsman Annual Reports)

### Takeaway
Complaints to the RBI Ombudsman reached 13.34 lakh in FY2024-25, up 13.55%. They had risen about 33% in FY2023-24 and have roughly doubled since FY2021-22. Loans and advances (29%) and credit cards (+20% YoY) are the top complaint areas. Ombudsman disposal fell to 93.07%, and pendency became a Governor-level concern. 37.84% of complaints handled at ORBIOs were non-maintainable. The largest single reason (33%) was customers going to the RBIO without first complaining to the bank. These numbers point to weak front-end guidance and weak internal redress at banks.

### Cited Findings
**FY2024-25 (RB-IOS 2021 Annual Report, published 1 Dec 2025)**
- Total complaints received: 13,34,244 in FY2024-25 vs 11,75,075 in FY2023-24, up 13.55%. — [RBI Annual Report of Ombudsman Scheme 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Channels: the CRPC (Chandigarh) received 9,11,384 (+18.83%), and the ORBIOs received 2,96,321 (+0.82%). 2,45,459 complaints were auto-closed in CMS as non-maintainable (2,40,720 the previous year). — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- The CRPC closed 7,76,336 complaints itself (85.20% of those it processed). 93.18% of CRPC receipts came by email, 5.78% by physical letter and 1.04% via CPGRAMS. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- 91.22% of complaints at ORBIOs came through digital modes (88.77% in FY24). Complaints per lakh accounts were 7.7 (8.9 in FY24). — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- By entity type: banks 81.53% (2,41,601) and NBFCs 14.80% (43,864). The private bank share rose from 34.39% to 37.53% (1,11,199). The PSB share fell from 38.32% to 34.80% (1,03,117). Small finance bank complaints rose 42.00%. NBPSPs had 5,617 and CICs 4,585 (up from 3,847). — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Categories: the top five categories made up 86.20% of complaints. Loans and advances were the largest at 29.25%. Credit card complaints rose 20.04% to become the second-largest category. Mobile/electronic banking complaints fell 12.74%. Breakdown of bank complaints: deposit accounts 49,833 (20.63%), mobile/electronic banking 46,700 (19.33%), credit cards 41,457 (17.16%), ATM/debit cards 18,042 (7.47%, down from 25,173). Mis-selling is not reported as a separate category. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- ORBIO disposal: 2,90,567 complaints disposed (93.07%). Maintainable: 1,80,621 (62.16%). Non-maintainable: 1,09,946 (37.84%, up from 32.17% in FY24 and 28.23% in FY23). — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Reasons for non-maintainable closure: first-resort complaints not filed with the RE first 36,289 (33.01%); already dealt with or pending at ORBIO (duplicates) 20,721 (18.85%); incomplete information 14,642 (13.32%); commercial judgement of the RE 9,374 (8.53%); addressed to other authorities 6,572 (5.98%); RE acted under LEA orders 5,292 (4.81%). — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Outcomes for maintainable complaints: mutual settlement, conciliation or mediation 93,752 (51.91%); rejected 78,323 (43.36%); withdrawn 8,510 (4.71%); awards 36 (0.02%). — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Pendency: 14,667 brought forward, 3,12,204 handled and 2,90,567 disposed. That leaves about 21,600 pending at year end (my derivation; the report does not state it). The report gives no average TAT in days. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Average cost to RBI per complaint was ₹1,582 (₹1,732 previously). For FY2024-25, ₹7.18 crore was recovered from 31 banks under the cost-recovery framework for banks with excess complaints. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Bank-group intensity, shown as complaints per branch / digital complaints per 1,000 digital transactions / non-digital complaints per 1,000 accounts: PSBs 1.11 / 0.00018 / 0.037; private banks 2.29 / 0.00048 / 0.079; SFBs 0.74 / 0.00066 / 0.046; payments banks 8.81 / 0.00017 / 0.018; foreign banks 9.44 / 0.00240 / 0.206. Credit card complaints per 1,000 cards: SFBs 1.141, foreign banks 0.923, private banks 0.420, PSBs 0.114. No bank is named individually. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Highest complaints per lakh accounts by state: Chandigarh 35.1, Delhi 10.2, Gujarat 10.2, Maharashtra 9.9. Lowest: Mizoram 1.5 and Nagaland 2.1. RBI notes low complaint levels from rural and semi-urban areas and ran a survey on why. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Contact centre (helpline 14448): 9,27,598 calls (+28.89%). Language split: Hindi 70.43%, regional languages 22.84%, English 6.73%. The service covers Hindi, English and 10 regional languages. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Appeals: 104 received and 53 disposed (13 in the complainant's favour), with 108 pending at year end. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- RBI urged REs to put complaint analyses before their Boards and to hold a monthly "Grievance Redress Day". It plans to upgrade the CMS in FY2025-26. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- Press coverage confirms the 13.55% rise, the fall in disposal to 93.07% from 95.10%, and the bank share of 81.5%. — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/rbi-ombudsman-disposal-falls-to-93-in-fy25-when-to-approach-for-complaint-redressal)

**FY2023-24 (previous report)**
- 9,34,355 complaints received at ORBIOs and the CRPC. 88.77% of ORBIO receipts were digital. Disposal was 95.1%. Banks accounted for 82.28% and NBFCs 14.53%. Loans and advances were the largest category at 29.01%. — [RBI AR 2023-24](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23126); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/india-news/ombudsman-disposed-of-95-of-complaints-received-in-fy24-says-rbi-125012401605_1.html)
- FY2022-23 received 7.03 lakh. The rise to 9.34 lakh is about 33% (reported as 32.81%, from a secondary source). — [IASGyan](https://www.iasgyan.in/daily-current-affairs/rbi-ombudsman-scheme)
- Moneylife reports roughly 50% CAGR in Ombudsman complaints over two years to FY24. It also reports that scheduled commercial banks received about 1 crore (10 million) complaints internally in FY24, excluding NBFCs. — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/will-rbi-governors-tough-talk-on-consumer-complaints-lead-to-real-change/76725.html)

**Pendency drive and new scheme**
- In the Dec 2025 policy statement, Governor Malhotra said pendency with the RBI Ombudsman had risen because of a large number of grievances. He announced a two-month campaign (1 Jan to end Feb 2026) to resolve all grievances pending more than a month. Banks and NBFCs had to review and dispose of complaints pending beyond 30 days. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/rbi-launches-two-month-campaign-to-clear-all-pending-customer-complaints-125120500258_1.html)
- RB-IOS 2026 replaced RB-IOS 2021 from 1 Jul 2026. The cap on consequential-loss compensation rises from ₹20 lakh to ₹30 lakh, and the cap for loss of time, harassment and mental anguish from ₹1 lakh to ₹3 lakh (₹33 lakh total). There is still no cap on the value of the dispute. A customer can escalate if the RE rejects the complaint, the customer is unsatisfied, or the RE does not reply within 30 days. — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/rbi-ombudsman-scheme-2026-customers-can-seek-compensation-of-up-to-rs-33-lakh); [Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/01/rbi-integrated-ombudsman-scheme-2026-key-changes/)

### Inferences
- The two totals for FY24 reconcile. The FY24 report counted 9,34,355. The FY25 report restates FY24 as 11,75,075, which equals 9,34,355 plus the 2,40,720 complaints auto-closed in CMS. Anyone quoting a trend should use the same basis for both years.
- About 2.45 lakh auto-closures, plus 36k first-resort rejections, plus 20.7k duplicates, are complaints that failed at the bank's front door. This is a direct product opportunity: capture every expression of dissatisfaction at the bank, acknowledge it, de-duplicate it, and track it against the 30-day clock before the customer goes to the RBIO.
- With 51.9% of maintainable complaints settled by mediation (the bank conceding once RBI is involved), many complaints could have been resolved internally. That supports auto-escalation to the IO and pre-RBIO review.
- Credit cards (+20%) and loans (29%) should be the top-priority taxonomies for any engine. Digital and mobile complaints are falling but remain about 19% of bank complaints.
- Payments banks and foreign banks have the highest complaints per branch, and SFBs the highest card complaint intensity. These segments may be the strongest early buyers.
- Regional-language demand is real: 22.84% of helpline calls are in regional languages.

### Gaps
- FY2025-26 Ombudsman report not yet published or found.
- No average TAT in days in the FY25 report. No bank-by-bank named data was found in the parts I read; the RBI publishes bank-level complaint data separately in annexures and disclosures, which I did not check.
- Compensation amounts paid via RBIO are not reported.
- Outcome of the Jan-Feb 2026 pendency drive not found.

## 2. RBI's stated concerns: speeches, supervisory criticism, cost recovery

### Takeaway
Since 2025 the RBI's top leadership has named specific failure modes in banks' grievance redress. These are: misclassifying complaints as queries or requests (a "gross regulatory violation"); rejecting complaints without senior review; bypassing the Internal Ombudsman (many complaints later decided for the customer at the RBIO had never gone to the IO); treating closure as resolution; no root cause analysis of repeat complaints; and weak Board oversight. RBI responded with mandatory auto-escalation to the IO (Jan 2026), a pendency drive, cost recovery from banks with excess complaints, and a new Ombudsman scheme.

### Cited Findings
- **Governor Sanjay Malhotra, 17 Mar 2025, Annual Conference of RBI Ombudsmen** (theme "Transforming Grievance Redress: The AI Advantage"):
  - He flagged REs misclassifying complaints as requests, queries or disputes. He said this leaves grievances unaddressed and is "a gross regulatory violation." — [RBI speech](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1498)
  - Requests should not be rejected without examination by a senior functionary empowered to decide in the consumer's interest. Banks need enough grievance redress officers at all levels, including in the IO office. — [RBI speech](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1498)
  - He called the high share of complaints needing Ombudsman intervention a "highly unsatisfactory situation". His proposals: track social-media complaints before they escalate, treat repeat complaints as systemic failures, two-tier IOs in large REs, AI-based complaint categorisation, and a revised 2014 Citizen's Charter with service timelines. Focus areas were KYC (most REs have not enabled CKYCR checks at branches), digital fraud, mis-selling and aggressive recovery. — [Moneylife summary](https://moneylife.in/article/will-rbi-governors-tough-talk-on-consumer-complaints-lead-to-real-change/76725.html)
- **Deputy Governor Swaminathan J, 13 Jul 2026, IO Conference, Mumbai** ("Strengthening Customer Grievance Redress: The Role of the Internal Ombudsman"):
  - "A significant proportion of complaints" that the RBI Ombudsman finally resolved in the customer's favour had never been referred to the IO. He called this a serious concern, and said complaints must be referred "promptly and without exception". — [RBI Bulletin speech](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=24304)
  - Complaint closure and complaint resolution are not the same thing. A case can be technically closed after a response while the problem stays open. Outcomes should be judged on timeliness, fairness and clear communication. — [RBI speech](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=24304)
  - "A hundred complaints relating to the same issue are seldom a hundred independent problems." He called for root cause analysis and fixes through process redesign, product changes, training and controls. — [RBI speech](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=24304)
  - Systems must not inadvertently bypass the IO through classification or process gaps. The IO must not become "merely another part of [the] internal approval chain." Boards should treat complaint trends as business intelligence. Analytics and early-warning indicators help, but technology "cannot replace judgment, fairness or empathy." — [RBI speech](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=24304); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/rbi-calls-for-stronger-internal-ombudsman-framework-across-regulated-entities)
- **Jan 2026 direction:** banks and eligible NBFCs must automatically escalate partially resolved or rejected complaints to the IO and give the final decision within 30 days. The IO handles only complaints the RE has already examined. Complaints must be tagged 'fully resolved', 'partially resolved' or 'wholly rejected' in the RE's CMS before escalation. — [Business Standard headline](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/rbi-mandates-auto-escalation-of-unresolved-complaints-to-internal-ombudsman-126011401228_1.html) (full text not accessible, 403); [Business Standard 2026 search summary](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/topic/ombudsman)
- **Earlier supervisory view (Kanungo Committee, 2023):** "an overwhelming percentage of rejected (fully/partly) complaints are not being referred to the IO by banks/REs". Fewer than a quarter were vetted by the IO. Board customer-service committees were described as largely ineffective. — quoted in [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/will-rbi-governors-tough-talk-on-consumer-complaints-lead-to-real-change/76725.html)
- **Historical root-cause view (DG S.S. Mundra, 2017):** he linked mis-selling to aggressive targets, incentive-linked quotas, poor training, fast staff rotation and poor front/back-office coordination. He urged root cause analysis through end-to-end automation of grievance redress and analytics. — [RBI speech Id=1564 via search summary](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1564)
- **Cost recovery:** ₹7.18 crore was recovered from 31 banks for FY2024-25 for complaint volumes above peer benchmarks. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- **Critics on enforcement:** Moneylife argues RBI has repeatedly flagged IO escalation failures (including in Jan 2024) with little change. It says KYC-based account freezes continue despite Kanungo's view, no penalties have followed misclassification, and AI is premature while banks still fail at basic complaint tracking. It also cites a reader with a complaint pending 84 days at the RBIO. — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/will-rbi-governors-tough-talk-on-consumer-complaints-lead-to-real-change/76725.html); [Moneylife column on non-maintainable closure](https://moneylife.in/article/rbi-and-sebi-postoffice-regulators-and-the-art-of-closing-complaints-without-inquiry/72318.html)

### Inferences
- The failure modes RBI names translate directly into engine features:
  - complaint-vs-query classifier with audit trail (misclassification)
  - mandatory senior or IO review gate before any rejection
  - auto-escalation to the IO at T+X days or on partial/rejected status
  - closure-quality checks on final replies (closure vs resolution)
  - root cause clustering of repeat complaints
  - Board-ready MIS
  - social-media complaint ingestion
- Cost recovery creates a direct financial incentive. A bank with fewer RBIO escalations pays less, which supports an ROI story.

### Gaps
- Exact quantification of "significant proportion" of complaints that bypassed the IO is not given in the 2026 speech.
- Full text of the Jan 2026 IO amendment circular could not be retrieved (403). Verify the 30-day timeline and scope on rbi.org.in.
- I could not confirm the AI-categorisation passage directly from the primary March 2025 speech text (it is reported by Moneylife).

RBI penalties repeatedly cite the same customer-protection rules. These are: not shadow-reversing disputed unauthorised e-transactions within 10 working days; not resolving complaints or paying compensation within 90 days; no 24x7 channel to report fraud; customer service directions generally; KYC; recovery-agent conduct; credit-card billing (late fees charged without sending statements); IO non-compliance; and late reporting of fraud and cyber incidents. Individual penalties are usually ₹20 lakh to ₹2.3 crore and often bundle several charges.

### Inferences
- The most frequent customer-facing breach is the limited-liability workflow: 10-working-day shadow credit, 90-day resolution and compensation, and a 24x7 reporting channel. It appeared in penalties on SBI, Central Bank (twice), Union Bank and GP Parsik. All of these are deterministic, clock-based obligations, so a rules engine with SLA timers and auto-compensation triggers could prevent them.
- The second cluster is IO non-compliance plus generic "customer service directions" (RBL, Muthoot FinCorp, PNB, IPPB). Escalation logic and audit trails address these.
- The remaining clusters are KYC (HDFC, IDFC First) and recovery-agent call-hour limits (HDFC, Kotak). These are adjacent modules: contact-time guardrails and KYC update tracking.
- Penalties come from inspections roughly 18-24 months after the reference date. That means the evidence trail (timestamps, compensation calculations) must be retained and retrievable for audit.

### Gaps
- No 2025-26 bank penalty specifically for not escalating rejected complaints to the IO was found.
- No comprehensive count of customer-service penalties per year; RBI's enforcement page would need a full scrape.
- Penalty amounts above are as reported. For some (HDFC ₹1 crore, Kotak) the primary RBI press release was not opened.

## 4. Operational pain points and committee findings (Kanungo Committee, industry)

### Takeaway
The Kanungo Committee (report dated April-June 2023) and later commentary describe a grievance system with these structural gaps: IO referral is low and the IO has conflicts of interest; channels are fragmented (calls for one common portal and fraud reporting through the cybercrime portal); mis-selling from cross-selling targets; repeated KYC demands; deceased-claim and lost-document delays; and the absence of a regulatory cost for poor service. RBI data adds duplicates, incomplete complaints, low rural voice and regional-language demand.

### Cited Findings
- The committee was set up on 23 May 2022, chaired by former DG B.P. Kanungo. Its report is dated 5 Jun 2023 (cover letter 24 Apr 2023). — [RBI report page](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationReportDetails.aspx?ID=1232); [RBI PDF](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/87730/39711208/ReportCustomerService0dc064a6613c40ab8343b94ef50dfa13.pdf)
- Recommendations: principle-based customer service regulation (fair treatment, transparency, suitability, data protection, grievance redress); "same activity, same regulation" across REs; an RE-agnostic common complaint portal; a review of the reasonableness of charges. — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/kanungo-committee-recommends-penalty-for-deficient-customer-service-audit-of-crossselling-among-others-for-rbi-regulated-entities/71009.html)
- IO independence: suggests an IBA- or RBI-administered fund to pay IOs, to remove the conflict of interest, with costs recovered from REs in proportion to complaints referred to their IOs. — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/kanungo-committee-recommends-penalty-for-deficient-customer-service-audit-of-crossselling-among-others-for-rbi-regulated-entities/71009.html)
- Mis-selling: the audit function should verify cross-sales of third-party products. — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/kanungo-committee-recommends-penalty-for-deficient-customer-service-audit-of-crossselling-among-others-for-rbi-regulated-entities/71009.html)
- Proposes a regulatory cost on REs with deficient customer service. — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/kanungo-committee-recommends-penalty-for-deficient-customer-service-audit-of-crossselling-among-others-for-rbi-regulated-entities/71009.html)
- Technology recommendations: a centralised KYC linked to a unique customer ID to stop repeated KYC; fraud reporting and complaints on the cybercrime portal; online settlement of deceased-account claims; a standardised ATM interface. — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/kanungo-committee-recommends-penalty-for-deficient-customer-service-audit-of-crossselling-among-others-for-rbi-regulated-entities/71009.html)
- Implementation lag: the committee's recommendation on lost property documents (affidavit-cum-indemnity plus a compensation formula) reportedly remains unimplemented. KYC-related account freezes continue. Board customer-service committees are largely ineffective. — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/will-rbi-governors-tough-talk-on-consumer-complaints-lead-to-real-change/76725.html)
- Data-quality symptoms in RBIO data: 18.85% of non-maintainable complaints were duplicates (already dealt with or pending), and 13.32% had incomplete information. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- The 2017 diagnosis of the root causes of mis-selling (targets, incentives, training, staff rotation) still frames the issue. Mis-selling and aggressive recovery remained Governor focus areas in 2025. — [RBI speech 2017](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1564); [RBI speech 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1498)

### Inferences
- Branch, email, call-centre, social-media and chatbot channels are not consolidated. The evidence is the Governor's call to track social-media complaints and the misclassification problem. A unified complaint register that ingests every channel and applies a regulator-aligned taxonomy is a core need.
- Manual TAT tracking is implied by the penalised misses of the 10-day, 30-day and 90-day clocks.

### Gaps
- No quantitative academic or industry survey of bank-internal complaint handling (e.g., % of complaints misclassified, chatbot failure rates) was found in this pass.
- Chatbot or AI failure evidence in Indian banks was not found from reliable sources.
- I did not read the full Kanungo PDF for its statistical annexes on internal complaint volumes, TATs and regional languages.

## 5. RBI SupTech and what it implies banks must provide

### Takeaway
RBI is digitising its side of grievance redress and fraud detection: the CMS portal (91% of receipts digital), CRPC auto-closure, a planned CMS upgrade, AI use in complaint redressal, the FREE-AI committee, and MuleHunter.AI in about 23-26 banks. This raises the bar for banks to supply clean, structured, timely complaint and fraud data, and to show AI governance.

### Cited Findings
- 2,45,459 complaints were auto-closed as non-maintainable in CMS in FY25. CMS upgradation is planned for FY2025-26. A committee on a FREE-AI framework for the financial sector was set up on 26 Dec 2024. — [RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)
- RBI's annual report highlights AI use in complaint redressal, fraud detection and internal workflows, including ChiRAG, its own generative AI platform. — [Moneycontrol via TradingView](https://vn.tradingview.com/news/moneycontrol:4f636e419094b:0-rbi-embraces-ai-to-tackle-fraud-and-enhance-efficiency)
- MuleHunter.AI (RBIH) was launched in Dec 2024 after a pilot in two PSBs, and the Finance Ministry urged banks to adopt it. Adoption: at least 15 banks (Aug 2025), about 20 (Governor, Nov 2025), 23 as of 10 Dec 2025 (RTI), and 26 per a later Lok Sabha reply (undated). RBIH claims 3x the accuracy of manual systems. RBI declined to disclose how many mule accounts were identified. — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-rti-23-banks-mulehunter-mule-accounts/); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/what-is-mulehunter-ai-rbi-s-latest-tool-against-financial-fraud-explained-124120600865_1.html); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/rbi/ai-tool-launched-to-detect-mule-accounts-used-in-cyber-fraud.html)
- The Governor proposed AI-based complaint categorisation and social-media complaint tracking (Mar 2025). — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/will-rbi-governors-tough-talk-on-consumer-complaints-lead-to-real-change/76725.html)

### Inferences
- As RBI's CMS analytics improve, the RBI can cross-check RBIO complaints against banks' own reported data, for example complaints the RBIO decided for the customer that never went to the IO. Banks will need data that holds up to that reconciliation: consistent taxonomy, timestamps, IO-referral flags and closure status.
- FREE-AI implies that any AI complaint classifier at a bank will need explainability, human review of rejections and audit logs. This fits the Governor's "senior functionary" review requirement.

### Gaps
- No public detail on DAKSH or CMS-analytics outputs relating to complaints was found in this pass.
- No specific RBI data submission format for complaint MIS (e.g., new returns under RB-IOS 2026) was verified.
